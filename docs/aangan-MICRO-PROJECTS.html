Do you need a Raspberry Pi?

You asked the right question. Here is the honest answer rather than the defensive one.

For the studio — the sound meter, the doors, presence, the recording verdict: no. You do not need one.

Each ESP32 already runs its own small web server. You saw it: Node 1 is live at 192.168.0.158 with no Pi anywhere. Every node can publish its readings straight onto your Wi-Fi, and the app can read them directly. Nothing is missing for the studio.

My recommendation: skip the Pi for now. Build the studio on Wi-Fi alone. Revisit the question the day you hit something that genuinely needs an always-on brain. When that day comes, try your studio Mac mini before spending ₹10,000 on a Pi.

The one thing I will not let you skip quietly. If the safety logic lives only in the app, then a gas leak at three in the morning with every phone locked means nothing happens. That is not a smaller version of the feature. It is the absence of it.

It is fixable without a Pi: an ESP32 can send a notification straight to your phones by itself, through a free push service, with no hub and no account. I have not built that yet, but it is a small job, and it is the piece that makes a Pi-free house honest rather than just cheaper.
Nothing you build now is a bet on this decision. These nodes work both ways. Every one you wire today serves its own page over Wi-Fi, and if you later add a brain, whether a Pi or the studio Mac, it adopts them as they are. No rewiring, no reflashing, no wasted work. So build, and decide the brain question later with real experience behind it.

The seven micro-projects

Ranked by what you said matters and what your team has actually shown up for. Each one is a finished, visible thing, not a step toward something distant.

1

The Quiet Meter

Live decibels from the recording room, on every phone. You told me this is the one that matters most, so it goes first.
ALL PARTS IN HAND SEN0232 + ESP32 HALF A DAY NO PI NEEDED
Finished looks like: open your phone, see a number that moves when someone speaks, and a clear verdict: quiet enough to record, or not.
The team's real job here is calibration, and it is the interesting part. The sensor gives a voltage; turning that into a trustworthy decibel number means comparing it against a phone sound-level app in a silent room, then during an actual noisy take, and recording what threshold really separates a usable take from a ruined one. That number cannot be looked up. Only your room can tell you.
2

The Door Board

Which of the four door leaves is open, named exactly. Already programmed and on your Wi-Fi — only the wiring is left.
ALREADY FLASHED & LIVE 4 reeds of 12 ONE HOUR NO PI NEEDED
Finished looks like: the page names the exact leaf someone left open, not just "a door".
Bench-test all four with the magnets before anything goes on a door frame. Permanent mounting waits on two-core cable, which was never ordered.
3

Is Anyone In There

Radar presence — detects a person sitting completely still, by their breathing. Shares a board with the Quiet Meter, so it is nearly free once #1 is done.
PART IN HAND LD2410 TWO HOURS NO PI NEEDED
Finished looks like: the studio knows it is occupied even when nobody moves, something a motion sensor never manages.
Needs tuning: radar sees through plasterboard and will happily report someone in the next room. Setting the distance limits is a genuine afternoon of experimenting.
4

The Green Light

The one that turns three sensors into a product: can I record right now? Doors shut, room quiet, someone present, nodes alive.
NEEDS 1 + 2 + 3 APP WORK, MINE ONE DAY NO PI NEEDED
Finished looks like: one glance before a take, and a named reason when the answer is no, such as "teaching door leaf B is open" rather than a red cross.
This is my job, not the team's. It is where Aangan stops being sensors and starts being the thing you actually use.
5

The Gas Cylinder Scale

Weighs the LPG cylinder so the app can say how much is left and roughly how many days. The most satisfying build on the list.
ALL PARTS IN HAND HX711 + 4 load cells NEEDS A PLATFORM BUILT
Finished looks like: "about 6.4 kg left, roughly 11 days" instead of finding out mid-cooking.
Somebody has to build a physical platform — two boards with the load cells sandwiched at the corners, then calibrate it with a known weight. Hands-on, visible, and a good project for whoever on the team likes making things rather than wiring them.
6

The Instrument Guard

Precise humidity in the rooms holding the piano and guitars, with an alert outside a safe band.
PARTS IN HAND 2 × SHT31 HALF A DAY
Finished looks like: a warning before the humidity does damage to instruments worth lakhs, not after.
Quick to build, but it needs weeks of readings before the alert thresholds mean anything. Start it early precisely because it takes time to become useful.
7

Water Watch

Eight leak probes under sinks, bathrooms, the geyser and the washing machine.
ALL PARTS IN HAND 9 probes, 8 used HOUSE, NOT STUDIO
Finished looks like: you hear about a leak in minutes rather than when the ceiling stains.
Repetitive but easy: the same two-wire pattern eight times. Good for a session where the team wants an obvious win. Three of the probes need a resistor each.
