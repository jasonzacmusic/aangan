Every one of these matches the plan quantity exactly. Combined with the six ESP32s already owned, there are now nine node brains for a six-node design — a healthy spare margin.
One thing to know on the bench. These are the 38-pin ESP32, not the 30-pin the list specified. Electrically it is the better board. But a 38-pin ESP32 is wider, and on an 830-point breadboard it covers the holes on both sides — leaving nowhere to land a jumper. Bridge two breadboards side by side, or use the perfboard directly. Not a fault, just a five-minute surprise on build day if nobody warns you.

The ADS1115 was a smart catch. Four MQ-6 sensors and the sound level meter all output an analogue voltage, and the ESP32's own converter is unusable on ADC2 pins whenever Wi-Fi is on — which is always. Two of these boards give eight clean 16-bit channels over I²C and sidestep that trap entirely. It was not on the final list; it should have been.
The memory card is the wrong one, and there is only one. What shipped is a SanDisk Ultra (A1). The plan called for three Extreme A2 cards — House Pi, Music Pi, and a spare. A2 is meaningfully faster at the small random reads an operating system does all day, and Home Assistant on an A1 card feels sluggish in a way people blame on the Pi. Buy three Extreme A2 64 GB and keep the Ultra as the spare.
Two small shortfalls. SW-420 vibration sensors: 4 shipped, 5 planned — buy one more (₹70). Jumper wires: male-to-male and male-to-female came, but no female-to-female, which is the kind you need most once modules are soldered onto perfboard. A 40-piece F-F bundle is about ₹120 on SP Road.

The power bank's headline wattage tells you nothing. A Pi 5 can only take 5 V, and most “65 W” banks deliver just 3 A at 5 V — the big numbers live at 9 V and 20 V, which the Pi cannot use. Find the 5 V line in the small print on the back. For it to work as a UPS it must also do pass-through charging: test by plugging the wall charger into the bank and the Pi into the bank, and confirming the Pi stays on while the bank charges.
Write both memory cards today — no Pi required. Raspberry Pi Imager on a laptop, hostname house on one card and studio on the other, SSH switched on and Wi-Fi entered in the same settings screen. Twenty minutes now removes the slowest step from build day.
