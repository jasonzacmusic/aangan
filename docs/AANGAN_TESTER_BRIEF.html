Why this exists

One flat is a family home: a mother, two brothers. The other is a studio where lessons are taught and videos are recorded for thousands of students. Those two lives collide constantly. A doorbell during a take ruins the take. A delivery partner at the gate mid-lesson means someone has to break the class. If someone in the family needs help, the person who can help might be wearing headphones in a soundproofed room.

So Aangan is not a gadget collection. It is one dial that tells the whole house what is happening — Available, Class, Recording, Emergency — and the house then behaves accordingly. Everything you'll test hangs off that idea.

The four new things — and the one that will surprise you

This week's build is about air. Not the "smells nice" kind — the kind you can measure and act on.

Start here, because you have lived this one.

You know the 2 p.m. lecture where everybody is yawning, nobody can focus, and you blame lunch or the lecturer? Sixty people in a closed room push carbon dioxide past 1,500 ppm. Fresh outdoor air is about 430. Above roughly 1,000 your thinking measurably slows down. You cannot see it, smell it, or taste it — and an air purifier does nothing about it, because a purifier filters dust, it does not remove CO₂. Only opening something does. That is the entire reason one of these four features exists.

Purifiers go quiet when tape rolls

dBA
What it does
The moment the studio switches to Recording, every air purifier drops to its lowest speed. When the studio is free again, each one returns to the exact mode it was in before — not a guess, not a default.
Why it matters
A purifier is a fan. A fan is noise. Noise lands on the recording and cannot be removed afterwards. Until now this was a thing a human had to remember, mid-session, every session.
Where to look
Home tab → the Air card header changes to “hushed for the take”.

The room is purged before a class

PM2.5
What it does
Twenty minutes before a lesson in the calendar, purifiers run flat out. The moment the class begins, they go quiet again.
Why it matters
Students arrive to clean air and a silent room. Doing it the obvious way — running purifiers during class — gets you clean air and a fan whining over the piano.
Where to look
Air card → 10 min or 20 min purge buttons, and the countdown that replaces them.

The house asks for fresh air

CO₂ ppm
What it does
When CO₂ climbs past your threshold, the phones and the wall screens say so — in words, not numbers: “Crack the door — the room is going stale.”
Why it matters
It is the invisible problem from the box above. In a soundproofed studio with the doors shut, it is worse than a classroom, because the room is sealed by design.
Where to look
Home tab, above the room cards. Put the studio into Class mode and wait about half a minute — you will watch the number climb and the banner appear on its own.

The instruments get a bodyguard

RH %
What it does
If a room sits outside 35–65% humidity for half an hour, the house warns the family.
Why it matters
A piano is wood and felt under tension. Bangalore's monsoon damp warps it, dry air cracks it, and the damage is slow, silent and expensive. Two cheap sensors protect instruments worth lakhs. This may be the most valuable rule in the app and it is the least dramatic.
Where to look
Air card → the RH column per room, and Settings → Air & instruments to move the safe band.

Your job today — about twenty minutes

Do these in order on your phone. After each one, note what you expected versus what happened.

  1. Open the app and just look for thirty seconds. Before touching anything: can you tell what state the studio is in, and what that means for a person standing at the door? If not, that is a finding — write it down.
  2. Turn the dial to Class mode (Command tab → Class mode). Watch the Home tab for the next minute. The CO₂ number should climb and a banner should appear by itself.
  3. Start a recording (Command tab → Video Rec, then press and hold the confirm button). Go to Home. The Air card should say “hushed for the take” and both purifiers should read Silent. Why do you think it makes you hold the button instead of tapping it?
  4. Now stand the studio down (Command tab → Wind down). Both purifiers should return to Auto — the mode they were in before, not off. Check that this actually happened.
  5. Run a 10-minute purge from the Air card, then stop it. The countdown should appear, then the purifiers should go back to Auto. Try starting a purge while recording — it should refuse. Does it tell you why clearly enough?
  6. Open the SOS page at /#/sos and raise one as “Amma”, then mark yourself safe. This is the page Jason's mother would use. Ask yourself honestly: could a sixty-year-old who dislikes phones do this in a panic? That is the whole test.
  7. Open the door screen at /#/display/front-house on a laptop if you can. This is what a Swiggy delivery partner reads at the gate. Scan the QR on it with your phone. Is the wording clear to someone in a hurry who did not design this?
  8. Check Pre-flight and Settings. Pre-flight is the go/no-go before recording. Notice that air is listed as advice, not as a blocker. Do you agree with that decision? Argue with us if you don't.

What kind of feedback is actually valuable

“I didn't understand this” is the most useful sentence you can send us.

It is not a sign you missed something. It is a bug in our design. You are the closest thing we have to a first-time user, and that makes your confusion the most expensive information in the room. Do not polish it away before telling us.

  • Where did you hesitate? Any moment you weren't sure what a button would do.
  • What word confused you? We wrote all of this — if a label reads like jargon, it is wrong.
  • What did you expect that didn't happen? Even if you were "wrong", your expectation is data.
  • Would you trust it? If this were your mother's SOS button, would you rely on it?
  • On your phone: anything cut off, too small to tap, or awkward with one thumb.
  • What's missing? You will think of something we haven't. Say it even if it sounds obvious.

Words you'll hit, so nobody has to pretend

Simulation (or "mock")
The app is pretending to be a house, so it can be built and tested before a single wire is run. Real sensors replace the pretend ones later, and the screens do not change.
Home Assistant
Free open-source software that will run on a Raspberry Pi in the flat and do the actual talking to sensors and appliances. It keeps working when the app is closed and the phones are asleep — that is deliberate.
ESP32
A ₹400 Wi-Fi microcontroller. One per room, with sensors soldered on, reporting to the Pi. You have probably used one in a college project; this is the same chip doing a job someone depends on.
ppm
Parts per million — how CO₂ is measured. 430 outdoors, 1,000 is the comfort line, 1,500 is a stuffy classroom.
PM2.5
Dust particles small enough to reach your lungs. This is the number Bangalore's air-quality apps show you.
RH
Relative humidity, as a percentage. Instruments want 35–65%.
Kiosk mode
A tablet locked to one full-screen page, so a wall screen cannot be poked into a browser or a home screen.
