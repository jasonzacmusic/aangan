Node 1 — wiring the four door switches

Studio & teaching doors · no soldering · about an hour with two people

Uses only parts already delivered · 5 jumper wires, 1 breadboard, 4 reed switches

A reed switch is two pieces

You have twelve pairs. Node 1 uses four.

When the two pieces are close together, the switch is closed — the door is shut. Pull them apart and it opens. That is the entire mechanism; there is no power and no electronics inside. Only the switch half needs wiring.

Every switch connects the same way

One wire to its own numbered pin, the other wire to ground. Four times.

Reed switches have no polarity. Either of a switch's two wires can go to the pin and the other to ground — it works both ways round. There is nothing to get backwards.

Bench first, doors afterwards

Nothing gets mounted until all four read correctly on the table.

  1. Sit the breadboard next to the ESP32. Leave the board plugged into the Mac by USB — that powers it.
  2. Take five male-to-female jumper wires. The socket end pushes onto the ESP32's pins; the pin end pushes into the breadboard.
  3. Run one jumper from each of 25, 26, 32, 33 into four separate rows of the breadboard. Keep them apart — a row is the short line of five holes.
  4. Run the fifth jumper from a GND pin to the long strip down the edge of the breadboard marked with a blue or black line. That strip is now your shared ground.
  5. For each reed switch: push one of its wires into the same row as one of the numbered jumpers, and its other wire anywhere along the ground strip.
  6. Now test. Hold the magnet against its switch, then pull it away. Do this for each of the four, one at a time, and check the right door name changes on screen. Note which reed you assigned to which door as you go.
What "correct" looks like. Magnet touching the switch reads closed. Magnet pulled away reads open. If one behaves backwards, its two wires are fine — tell me and I will flip it in the firmware in thirty seconds. Do not rewire anything.
Two things to avoid. Never push a wire into the pins marked 3V3, 5V or VIN — those carry power, and joining one to ground shorts the board. And do not mount anything on a door until all four have passed on the table; a wiring fault is easy to find on a bench and miserable to find up a ladder.

On the doors

Both the studio and teaching rooms have double doors. Each door gets two reeds — one per leaf — so the app can tell you exactly which leaf was left open. Mount them at the meeting stiles: the middle, where the two leaves come together. Switch on the fixed side, magnet on the moving side, within about a centimetre of each other when shut.

The honest blocker. Reed switches come with short leads, and the two-core cable to run from a door back to the board was never ordered. So unless the ESP32 sits right beside a door, permanent mounting on both rooms waits for cable. Everything up to that point — build, wire, test, label — you can finish today.
