The entire job

Press the button marked BOOT. Let go.

Your ESP32 has two small buttons on it. One says EN (or RST). The other says BOOT (sometimes IO0 or FLASH). Press the BOOT one — a normal press, like a keyboard key — hold about a second, then release.

That button is already wired to a pin and to ground inside the board. Pressing it is electrically identical to a door reed switch closing. Nothing to touch, nothing to short, nothing you can get wrong.

Buttons sit at the opposite end from the USB socket on most boards, one at each corner. If yours are placed differently, the printed labels still say EN and BOOT — go by the label, not the position.
What happens next. Nothing visible on the board — no light will change. The proof appears on my side: I am reading its log live and will see BUTTON PRESSED the moment you do it, then BUTTON RELEASED. Just press it and tell me. That single press proves your Mac, the programming toolchain, the board and its input path all work — before the Raspberry Pi has even been bought.

The wire version, on the real door pin

The button proves the same thing. Do this only if you're curious, or when the reed switches are in your hand.

What you need

Anything metal that reaches between two pins

  • Best: two male-to-female jumper wires — push a socket end onto each pin, then twist the two pin ends together.
  • Fine: a paperclip bent into a narrow U.
  • Fine: metal tweezers, touching both pins at once.
  • Will not work: a single male-to-female jumper. The board's pins are male, so you need a socket at both ends — which is why the female-to-female pack I flagged as missing actually matters.
Which two pins

Find the label 25 and the nearest GND

Every pin is labelled in tiny white print along the edge of the board. Look for 25 (it may read D25, G25, GPIO25 or IO25 — all the same pin). Then find any pin marked GND; there are several, and any of them works. Touch your metal across those two, hold a second, remove.

The one rule. Only ever bridge a numbered pin to a GND pin. Never touch anything to the pins marked 3V3, 5V or VIN — bridging a power pin to ground is a short circuit and can kill the board instantly. If the print is too small to read with confidence, stop and send me a clear photo of the board with the labels visible; I will mark the exact two pins for you.
