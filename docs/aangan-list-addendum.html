The three lists are good and cleared to order. Silverline, SP Road and Amazon are internally consistent, the house side is complete, and the audio board is settled. This sheet records that decision and adds three cheap items worth putting on the SP Road order.

1 · The audio board — decided — Silverline list unchanged

DECIDED — keep the DAC Pro + XLR board. Order the Silverline list exactly as written. HiFiBerry has no Indian distributor, and this is the better board for the job anyway. Nothing below changes; this section is now a record of the decision and the three checks that still apply.

The decision — DAC Pro + XLR board (chosen)

Native balanced XLR out, no DI boxes, first-party Raspberry Pi board with the best driver support. The Pi becomes a dedicated Pianoteq stage piano — and native balanced output is exactly what a stage instrument feeding a console wants.

What this means for Chorale: it stays on the Mac, where it already works, is notarized, and measured 13% CPU. That was never a compromise — moving it to a Pi was an aspiration, not a need.

It forecloses nothing. If you later want Chorale on its own Pi, you buy a second Pi with an ADC board then. A Pi 5 is ₹8–10k — cheaper than degrading the piano rig's output today to keep a future option open.

The footswitch still works. The Pico pedal and the CC map are USB-MIDI, so they drive Chorale on the Mac identically. None of that work is wasted.
Three checks before the Silverline order goes in.

1 · Is the XLR daughter board actually in stock? This is the one genuine unknown in the whole build — it is a low-volume item. Ask specifically for the XLR board for the Raspberry Pi DAC Pro, and ask whether they can order it in if not.

2 · If it cannot be sourced: buy the DAC Pro anyway and feed its RCA output into a passive stereo DI box (Behringer DI400P ×2, ≈₹2,000 on Amazon). You still get true balanced XLR into the console. That fallback is genuinely good, not a downgrade.

3 · Pre-soldered headers? Newer DAC Pro revisions ship with bare pads instead of a fitted 6-pin female header on P7/P9. Ask which revision they are shipping — if it is the bare-pad version, the 2 headers already on the SP Road list cover it, plus five minutes of soldering.

5 · One wiring note for the builder

The kitchen node carries four MQ-6 sensors. Each has a heater drawing roughly 150 mA, so run all four heaters from the 5 V rail, never through the ESP32's 3.3 V regulator — it cannot supply 600 mA and will brown out.

Keep their analogue outputs on ADC1 pins (GPIO 32–39). ADC2 stops working the moment Wi-Fi is enabled on an ESP32, and this node is Wi-Fi.
