How to read this

GND means any pin marked GND. There are several on every board and they are all the same — use whichever is nearest.

3V3 and 5V are different and not interchangeable. Where a sensor is listed as 5V it will not work properly on 3V3. Where it says 3V3, giving it 5V can damage the ESP32's input.

All sensor grounds must be joined to the ESP32's ground, even when a sensor has its own power supply. Without a shared ground nothing reads correctly.
Never connect 3V3, 5V or VIN straight to GND. That is a short circuit and kills the board.

The LD2410's TX and RX cross over. Its transmit goes to the ESP32's receive. If presence never works, swap those two wires — it is the single most common mistake with this part.
Firmware DONE is not the same as a live reading. Temperature/humidity stay NA until the SHT31 header is soldered. Sound stuck at ~7.1 dBA means GPIO34 is empty — that number is invented by an unconnected pin, not a quiet room.

The MQ-6 gas sensors are the only part of this whole build that needs soldering. Each one needs two resistors: sensor AO through a 10kΩ to the ESP32 pin, then a 20kΩ from that pin down to GND. Without them the sensor can push 5V into a 3.3V input and destroy it.

Their heaters draw about 150 mA each — 600 mA for four. Run them from a separate 5V supply, not the ESP32, and join that supply's ground to the ESP32's ground.
Do not aim the flame sensor at the hob — it will alarm every time you cook.
The PIR needs 5V, not 3V3. It is the one sensor on this board with that requirement.

Is the code done?

Yes — 34 of 36 sensors are written, compiled and already flashed onto the boards. Nothing on this page needs programming. Plug a sensor in as shown and it appears on that board's web page within seconds.
Two are deliberately switched off, because their hardware does not exist yet:

Music room sound level — needs the second SEN0232 meter (₹4,799), never bought.
LPG detector contact — the Hikvision detector has arrived and it does have a relay output, so this one can be switched on now. It needs board 5 plugged back into the Mac for two minutes.

They are commented out rather than left active on purpose: an input with nothing wired to it reports a confident, believable reading that is completely false.
What still needs doing after wiring — and it can only be done afterwards: confirming which way the door reeds read, setting the decibel level that means "too loud to record", tuning how far the presence radar looks, giving the MQ-6 sensors 24–48 hours powered to settle, and calibrating the cylinder scale against a known weight. All of those I do remotely over Wi-Fi — no cables, a minute or two each.
