Short answer: yes to all three. (1) Your existing Dyson can join Aangan for ₹0 — it has excellent sensors inside it that we're currently wasting. (2) For other rooms, do not buy Dyson again — Xiaomi purifiers integrate natively for about a quarter of the price. (3) The Pi doesn't purify air; it decides — and that decision layer (recording-aware, class-aware, CO₂, odour, humidity) is worth more than any single purifier.

1 · The Dyson you already own ₹0 · the biggest win in this document

Wi-Fi Dyson purifiers speak MQTT, and Home Assistant talks to them through the community ha-dyson integration (installed via HACS). After a one-time sign-in it runs fully locally — no cloud in the daily loop.

What we gain immediately

  • Its sensors become the house's sensors. The Dyson already measures PM2.5, PM10, VOCs, NO₂, temperature and humidity to a standard better than most DIY parts. That data flows straight into Aangan's House Pulse — a professional air sensor we already paid for and currently can't see.
  • Full control: fan speed, auto mode, night mode, oscillation, plus filter life remaining (so the app can say “order a filter” instead of you discovering it).
  • It joins the studio logic. This is the real prize — see the automations below.
One honest caveat, resolved in five minutes: Dyson removed the local Wi-Fi credential sticker on models from roughly mid-2021 onward, so those need a single one-time MyDyson cloud login for the integration to fetch credentials — after that it's local. Very recent models occasionally lag support. Send me the model number from the sticker under the base (e.g. TP07, HP09, PH04) and I'll confirm support before we count on it. Cost either way: nothing.

2 · Purifiers for the other rooms the affordable answer

A second Dyson is roughly ₹45,000–55,000. It is a superb machine and it belongs where you record and teach — which is where you already have it. For bedrooms and the teaching room, the sensible buy is Xiaomi: native Home Assistant support (the built-in Xiaomi integration, local control), real HEPA + activated-carbon filtration, and about a quarter of the price.

Do not use the smart-plug trick on a purifier. Cutting power to a “dumb” purifier looks clever, but most units do not resume their previous speed when power returns — some need a physical button press, so the house silently stops purifying and nobody notices. Buy units the system can actually talk to (Dyson / Xiaomi). The one appliance where a plug or relay IS correct is a simple exhaust fan, which has no state to lose.

3 · The sensing layer where the Raspberry Pi earns its place · ~₹6–8k per room

A purifier only knows the air at its own grille, and only reacts. The Pi's job is to know the whole flat and act ahead of the problem. Each air node is one ESP32 (six already owned) plus these sensors, reporting to the House Pi over Wi-Fi:

SensorMeasuresWhy it matters here
PMS5003PM2.5 / PM10 dustBangalore road dust, and dust that visibly shows up in video
SCD41 (true CO₂, not an estimate)CO₂ ppmThe one everybody misses. A soundproofed room with the doors shut and four people in it climbs past 1,400 ppm fast — that is exactly why students get dull and heads get heavy in the second half of a class. You cannot smell it. Only a sensor finds it.
SGP41VOC index + NOx indexThis is the “smell” number — a live staleness/odour index. Cooking, paint, damp, bathroom, a closed room after a long day.
SHT45 (or the owned DHT22)temperature / humiditymould risk in monsoon and instrument protection — see §5

Where to put the three nodes: ① music room / studio (the room that earns money) ② kitchen (cooking smoke, odour, and it doubles as backup to the gas sensors) ③ Amma's bedroom (night-time CO₂ and humidity).

4 · The automations — this is the actual product

Gadgets on their own get ignored within a month. These rules are what make it a system, and every one of them is a rule the app can show and you can override.

Recording-aware purifying ★A purifier is a fan — it is noise on the take. When the dial goes Audio Rec or Video Rec, the Dyson drops to silent or off and joins the existing “Silence the room” pre-flight; when the studio returns to Available it goes back to purifying at full. Right now this is a manual thing you have to remember mid-session.Dyson integration

5 · “Anything for that matter” — the wider environment layer

The one I would insist on: instrument climate

Pianos want roughly 40–60% relative humidity and hate swings. Bangalore's monsoon pushes indoor humidity well past that, and the studio is sealed. Two humidity sensors and a rule that shouts early is insurance on instruments worth lakhs — and if it turns out a room sits damp for weeks, the fix is a dehumidifier on a relay (dehumidifiers, unlike purifiers, do resume after power returns — we verify the specific model first).

Also worth doing, cheap

  • RO water quality — a TDS probe on the drinking line (~₹600) tells you the RO filter is finished before the water tastes wrong.
  • AC efficiency nudge — “AC running with the balcony door open” is a rule we can write today from sensors already on order.
  • Circadian light — the WS2812B strip you own can warm down after sunset; smart bulbs later if you like the effect.
  • Kitchen chimney reminder — cooking dust spike with no chimney running → a nudge to switch it on.
  • Balcony rain + plants — the owned rain sensor and soil-moisture sensor already cover “bring the clothes in” and “the planter is dry”.
Deliberately NOT recommending: ozone generators or “ionisers” for odour (ozone is a lung irritant — never in an occupied room, and definitely not near singers), and cheap all-in-one “air quality” modules that estimate CO₂ from VOCs instead of measuring it. If we are going to act on a number, the number has to be real.
