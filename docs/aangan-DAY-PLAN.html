Tomorrow, step by step

Assume you have never done this before. Nothing here is skipped or assumed.

First: the electrician question

You do not need an electrician tomorrow. Not for any of it.

Everything you are doing runs on 5 volts from a phone charger — the same voltage as a USB cable. You cannot get a shock from it and you cannot damage the house with it. Nothing goes inside a wall, nothing connects to mains wiring, nothing needs a licence.

The only jobs that ever need a licensed technician are the panic-button loop, the geyser switching and the water pump. None of those are happening tomorrow, or this month. They are a separate project with separate parts you have not bought.

Your gas detector plugs into a normal socket like a phone charger. Your smoke alarms run on AA batteries. Neither needs an electrician either.

The one rule that protects the boards

Along the edges of the ESP32 you will see tiny white labels: numbers like 25, 26, 34, and words like GND, 3V3, 5V, VIN.

Sensors always connect between a NUMBERED pin and a GND pin. That is the pattern, every time, on every board.

Never join 3V3, 5V or VIN directly to GND. Those carry power. Connecting one straight to ground is a short circuit and kills the board instantly. If you are not sure what a pin says, stop and photograph it for Jason rather than guessing. A number may be printed as 25, D25, G25 or IO25 — all the same pin.
You cannot hurt yourself. The worst possible outcome is a dead ₹400 board, and you have three spares. Nothing here is dangerous to a person.

Board 1 · Studio — do these in order

Add one sensor. Test it. Only then add the next. If something stops working you will know exactly which step caused it.

1
Power it up. Nothing else.
Plug the micro-USB cable into the board and the charger into the wall. A small red light comes on.
Test: open http://192.168.0.158 on your phone. You should see a page titled Studio with a list of sensor names. If this does not load, stop and tell Jason. Nothing else will work until it does.
2
Sit the board on the breadboard
Push the ESP32 gently into the breadboard so its pins go into the holes, straddling the channel down the middle. If it will not fit, leave it beside the breadboard instead and use jumper wires — it works either way.
3
First door sensor — pin 25
A reed switch is two white bars. One has two wires, the other has none. The one with wires is the switch. Connect one wire to pin 25 and the other wire to any GND pin. It does not matter which way round.
Test: hold the plain white bar (the magnet) against the switch, then pull it away, while watching Studio door leaf A on your phone. It must change. Write down which way it went — magnet on = ? and magnet off = ? Jason needs that.
4
Second door sensor — pin 26
Exactly the same, on pin 26 instead.
Test: Studio door leaf B changes, and leaf A does not. If both move together, the two are touching — separate them.
5
The sound meter — the important one
The black board labelled Gravity Sound Level Meter. It has three wires: red to 5V, black to GND, and the third wire to pin 34.
Test: watch Studio sound level on your phone. Stay silent — note the number. Now clap or talk loudly — it must jump up, then settle. Write down the quiet number and the loud number. That is the measurement Jason needs to set the recording threshold, and only your room can provide it.
6
The presence radar
The small green board with a silvery pattern printed on it. Four wires: 5V, GND, then its TX to pin 16 and its RX to pin 17. Those two cross over — TX goes to 16, RX goes to 17.
Test: stand still in front of it for twenty seconds. Studio presence should stay on the whole time. Walk right away from it and it should eventually go off. It will probably detect people in the next room too — that is normal and Jason tunes it later.
7
The humidity sensor
Tiny board with four pins marked VIN, GND, SCL, SDA. VIN to 3V3 (not 5V), GND to GND, SDA to pin 21, SCL to pin 22.
Test: Studio temperature and Studio humidity should show real numbers within a minute. Breathe on it gently — humidity rises. If they stay blank, the four wires are in the wrong order.
8
The leak probe — last
Two pieces joined by a cable: a flat comb and a small board with a blue knob. The small board has pins: VCC to 3V3, GND to GND, DO to pin 27.
Test: touch the flat comb with a damp cloth. Studio sink leak must change. Dry it and it goes back. Use a damp cloth, never a running tap.
When all eight are done: photograph the breadboard from directly above, photograph the phone screen showing everything working, and send both to Jason with your three written numbers — door direction, quiet dB, loud dB. That is the studio finished.

If something does not work

What you seeWhat it usually means
The page will not load at allThe board is not on Wi-Fi. Unplug it, wait five seconds, plug it back in. Still nothing after two minutes — tell Jason.
A sensor never changesOne of its wires is in the wrong hole, or in the row next to the right one. Check the pin number again.
A sensor is stuck ONUsually its two wires are touching each other somewhere.
It works backwardsPerfectly normal. Do not rewire it. Write down what it does and Jason flips it in the software in under a minute.
Numbers look madExpected on anything not yet connected. Ignore sensors you have not wired.
The board gets warmNormal. Hot enough to hurt is not — unplug it and tell Jason.
Nothing you do here is permanent. No holes, no glue, no soldering. If it goes wrong, pull the wire out and start that step again. Photograph anything confusing rather than guessing.
