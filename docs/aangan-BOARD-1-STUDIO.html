Take exactly these six out of the pile

Leave everything else in its bag. These are the only parts board 1 uses.

Do this first — or you will run out of pins

Six sensors need six grounds. The ESP32 does not have six GND pins.

The breadboard has four long strips running down its edges — two at the top, two at the bottom. Each pair is marked with a red + line and a blue − line. These are called power rails, and every hole in one strip is connected to every other hole in that same strip.

Set them up once, then every sensor just reaches the nearest hole. Use three jumper wires:

Three setup wires, before any sensor:
1. ESP32 3V3 → top red rail
2. ESP32 5V → bottom red rail
3. ESP32 GND → top blue rail, then a short wire joining top blue to bottom blue

Now anything needing 3.3 volts goes to a top red hole, anything needing 5 volts to a bottom red hole, and every ground goes to any blue hole. You will never run out.
Get this the right way round. 3V3 and 5V must never touch each other, and neither must ever touch a blue rail. If you plug the two red rails into each other, or a red into a blue, the board dies instantly. Check these three wires twice before plugging in the USB.

Connect them in this order, testing after each

Never add two sensors between tests. If something breaks you want to know which one did it.

1

Door sensor A — the easiest

Two white bars. Only the one with wires gets connected. The plain one is a magnet and touches nothing.
wire 1  →  GPIO25
wire 2  →  any blue rail hole
no power · no polarity · either wire either way
Test: hold the magnet against the switch, then pull it away, watching Studio door leaf A on your phone. It must change. Write down which way round it goes.
2

Door sensor B

wire 1  →  GPIO26
wire 2  →  any blue rail hole
Test: Studio door leaf B changes and leaf A does not. If both move together their wires are touching.
3

Sound meter — the one that matters most

Black board marked Gravity Sound Level Meter V1.0 with a microphone. Its cable has three coloured wires.
red    →  bottom red rail (5V)
black  →  blue rail (GND)
blue/signal  →  GPIO34
Test: watch Studio sound level. Stay silent and note the number. Clap, and it must jump then settle. A stuck 7.1 (or anything that never moves) means GPIO34 is still empty. A working quiet reading is roughly 30–50. Write down both numbers — that is how the recording threshold gets set, and only your room can give it.
4

Presence radar

Small green board with a silvery printed rectangle — that pattern is the antenna. It has five pins; you use four.
VCC  →  bottom red rail (5V)
GND  →  blue rail
TX on the radar  →  GPIO16
RX on the radar  →  GPIO17
Test: stand still in front of it for twenty seconds — Studio presence must stay on the whole time. If it never comes on at all, swap the TX and RX wires. That is the usual fault and it harms nothing.
5

Temperature and humidity

Tiny purple or blue board. Four pins printed VIN, GND, SCL, SDA. It may have come with the pin strip loose in the bag.
VIN  →  top red rail (3V3)  not 5V
GND  →  blue rail
SDA  →  GPIO21
SCL  →  GPIO22
Test: Studio temperature and Studio humidity show real numbers within a minute. Breathe gently on it and humidity rises. NA is expected until the four header pins are soldered — the chip shipped unwelded, and jumpers on a loose strip do not count. Wrong wire order also stays NA.
6

Leak probe — last

Two pieces joined by a cable. The flat comb goes on the floor later; the small board with the blue knob stays dry and is the part you wire.
VCC  →  top red rail (3V3)
GND  →  blue rail
DO   →  GPIO27
AO is not used — leave it empty
Test: touch the flat comb with a damp cloth. Studio sink leak changes. Dry it and it returns. A damp cloth, never a running tap.
When all six are done: photograph the breadboard from directly above, photograph the phone screen showing every sensor live, and send both to Jason with your written numbers — door direction, quiet dB, loud dB.

Doors read backwardsNormal. Do not rewire. Write it down and Jason flips it in the software.
Everything stopped at onceUnplug immediately. Usually a red rail touching a blue rail.
Nothing here is permanent. No soldering, no glue, no holes. Pull a wire out and redo the step. Photograph anything confusing rather than guessing.
