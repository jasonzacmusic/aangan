The idea

One dial runs the house

Aangan is built around six studio states. Whoever is running the studio turns a dial in the app, and the door light and the screen outside change together. Anyone in the hall knows whether to walk in without knocking.

AvailableCOME INDim green. The studio is free.
ClassCLASSAmber. A lesson is running.
MeetingMEETINGAmber. Please wait.
Audio recON AIRSolid red. Recording, do not enter.
Video recFILMINGSolid red. A shoot is on.
EmergencyEMERGENCYViolet, flashing. Do not enter.
Fast red flashA studio door opens while a take is running. Someone is about to walk into shot.
Slow red pulseThe room gets too loud for a take, measured by the sound meter inside the studio.

These two rules depend on the studio sensor board (board 1) and have not yet been confirmed on real hardware. The six states themselves were tested end to end on 22 Aug.

How it is put together

Small Wi-Fi boards, one shared state

Every device in Aangan is an ESP32: a small, cheap computer board with Wi-Fi built in, about the size of a matchbox. Each runs one job and is powered from an ordinary USB phone charger. Nothing at the door is wired to anything else.

Setting the studio state
You, in the app
Turn the dial on a phone, laptop or iPad. Works from anywhere, even on mobile data.
The door service
Holds the one true state. aangan.nathanielschool.com/api/door
Light + screen
Both ask the service for the state every few seconds and change to match.
Door open, too loud
Studio board (board 1)
Door switches on both studio doors, the sound meter, the sink leak probe.
Straight to the door, chip to chip
Uses ESP-NOW, a direct radio link between ESP32s that skips the router. That is why a door opening can flash the light instantly, even if the Wi-Fi is down.

Why the devices ask the cloud instead of the app talking to them directly: the app is a secure (https) website, and browsers will not let a secure page talk to a plain device on the home network. Turning the arrow round, so the devices reach out, is what lets the dial work from anywhere.

ESP32 or Raspberry Pi?

Where the Pi fits

Aangan runs entirely on ESP32 boards. There is no Raspberry Pi in the house system, and that was a deliberate choice.

Jason decided the Pi stays reserved for music work. The original plan had a Pi acting as a hub for the whole house; it was never bought for that, and the system was redesigned so nothing depends on it.

The Pi work you may see in the project is separate. On 1 Sep a one-time setup script was prepared to turn a Pi into a music workstation (remote access, the coding tools, a shared folder for samples), and there is a plan for a piano Pi with a sound card that has not been bought. The Pi has never been plugged in, so none of that has run yet. Both Pis are parked until the music side needs them.

For the team

How to use it

  1. Open Aangan. aangan.nathanielschool.com Turn the dial on the Command screen. The light and the screen follow within a few seconds.
  2. Find the door page. In Aangan, open More, then Studio door. You can set the state and type a short message for the door, such as "Back at 4".
  3. Check both devices are on. The small badge at the top of Aangan shows two dots, strip and screen. Green means that device has checked in. Grey or red means it is off or unplugged.
  4. Put the sign on an iPad. Open the door sign in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Set Auto-Lock to Never so it stays on.
  5. Send it to a new teammate. The team page has both links as QR codes.

Anyone with the link can change the door. There is no password, so keep the links inside the team.

What is left

Getting from here to finished

  1. Plug the door light and the screen back in.anyone · 5 min
    Each needs a 5V USB charger and the house Wi-Fi, nothing else. Then check the two dots in Aangan turn green.
  2. Fit an always-on socket above the studio door.electrician · 30 min
    Replace the tube light on the G1 hall side with a 5A socket on an unswitched feed, so nobody can switch the recording light off by accident.
  3. Make the wooden bezel.carpenter
    A frame that sets the screen into the wood and hides the light strip in a routed channel.
  4. Load the latest program onto the studio board.needs the Mac · 5 min
    Board 1 must be re-programmed once so its door and noise signals reach the door light. Until then, the flash and pulse rules cannot work.
  5. Wire the room sensors.team + soldering help
    Most sensors are push-fit. The two climate sensors arrived with loose pins and need soldering first.
  6. Connect the real room readings to the app.development
    This is the largest remaining job. Until it is done, the app's room readings and the "ready to record" check run on demo data.
  7. Phone alerts with the app closed.development
    So a leak at 3 a.m. wakes someone. Designed, not built. This is the main safety gap.

Aangan is our studio and house system. One dial in the app sets the studio state (Come in, Class, Meeting, On Air, Filming, Emergency), and the light above the studio door and the screen beside it change together.

*Live now:* the app at aangan.nathanielschool.com and the cloud service behind it.
